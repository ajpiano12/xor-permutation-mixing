# Research map and literature review

**Question.** Fix one public permutation $P$ on $N=2^n$ points. Draw a fresh independent uniform XOR key each round and apply $P$ after the XOR. From every ordered tuple of $t$ distinct inputs, how close is the output to uniform after $r$ rounds? Problem 4 asks in particular for efficiently checkable sufficient conditions with explicit round counts. A decision about eventual mixing, a typical-table theorem, and a bound for an individual table answer different parts of this question.

## Where the project stands

| Question | Current answer | Evidence and attribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which orders eventually mix for a given table? | Decide all $t$ in time polynomial in the table size using a translation normal closure and stabilizer chain. At $n\ge3$, passing order four implies passing all orders through $N-2$. | [Group argument and implementation](ALGORITHMIC_PRIOR_ART.md), corollaries of permutation-group theory |
| How common is pairwise failure? | Exact at 3, 4 and 5 bits. At five bits the failure probability is about one in 9,694,845. The proof counts every preserved coset-partition obstruction at arbitrary $n$. | [Exact recurrence](FIVE_BIT_COUNT.md), project derivation using a published obstruction and the degree-32 classification |
| Why does the failure fraction change so quickly? | At large $n$, nearly every failing table preserves one fixed parity split. A single split is preserved with probability $2/\binom{N}{N/2}$. There are $N-1$ splits. Their overlaps and other failures have smaller order. | [Asymptotic equivalent, uniform in $2\le t\le N-2$](GROUP_COUNTS.md), project derivation |
| How many rounds for a typical fixed table? | With probability tending to one, exactly three cipher rounds for pairs at every fixed threshold strictly between zero and $e^{-1/2}$. | [Proof and finite samples](RATES_AND_LITERATURE.md), project derivation. Earlier authors analyzed eventual convergence and the one-round law |
| What can be certified for an explicit table? | A near-linear-in-table-size test for quadratic APN permutations gives pairwise TV at most $\tfrac12\sqrt{(N-2)/N^{r-1}}$. A separate comparison with a field inverse gives a three-round guarantee for triples even for tables differing in some entries. | [Pairs](QUADRATIC_CERTIFICATE.md) · [Triples](TRIPLE_ROUNDS.md), corollaries of known algebraic structures |
| Does mixing triples mean mixing quadruples? | At three bits, 5,376 labeled tables mix triples but preserve a family of planes on quadruples. Exact six-threshold times are available for every order. In larger dimensions even pairs and triples can separate. | [Exhaustive census](HIGHER_CENSUS.md) · [Explicit families](ALGORITHMIC_PRIOR_ART.md), project computations and examples |
| Do typical triples mix in four rounds? | At TV $1/4$, exactly four cipher rounds with probability tending to one. A collision calculation proves the upper bound for every start. A separate occupancy calculation shows three rounds miss at least $1/3-o(1)$ of the distribution in TV. | [Upper bound](TRIPLE_MOMENT.md) · [Lower bound](TRIPLE_LOWER.md) · [Experiment](TRIPLE_ROUNDS.md#what-a-random-table-does) |

## Closest work on the same walk

- **Independent subkeys and fixed round maps.** Lai, Massey and Murphy develop the Markov model for differential propagation. Hornauer, Stephan and Wernsdorf connect periodicity to fixed-length round groups and prove a sufficient condition using the alternating group. Vaudenay’s decorrelation theory develops tuple-distribution matrices under composition. These are direct predecessors of the difference walk and group algorithm. [Lai–Massey–Murphy, 1991](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-46416-6_2) · [Hornauer–Stephan–Wernsdorf, 1993](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-48285-7_41) · [Vaudenay, 2003](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00145-003-0220-6)
- **O’Connor and Golić.** In ASIACRYPT 1994, §3, Theorem 5 and Corollary 2, the authors establish a density bound and deduce typical eventual convergence under a stated random-graph assumption. That assumption would require a transfer to the dependent support graph of a uniform permutation. O’Connor’s EUROCRYPT 1995 paper (§2.2) explicitly examined all 40,320 three-bit bijections, finding 10,752 differentially 2-uniform (APN) maps, all ergodic. Section 3 uses the row Gram matrix to study convergence. Example 3 computes it for the three-bit inverse. The one-step random difference-table distribution also predates this project. Our pairwise three-round proof controls the actual uniform-permutation law and simultaneously covers all starts. The general positive two-step kernel for APN maps and its faster crooked specialization are treated in the certificate note. [O’Connor–Golić, full paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2FBFb0000450.pdf) · [O’Connor, full paper](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007%2F3-540-49264-X_2.pdf) · [O’Connor, one-step law](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00190756) · [Golić, §2](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/17/ocr-ajc-v17-p147.pdf)
- **The paper named in the assignment.** Liu, Tessaro and Vaikuntanathan study statistical $t$-wise independence, including actual inverse and cubing functions. Section 4.5 permits different public permutations in its existence argument. A theorem for typical sequences of round permutations does not by itself apply on the diagonal $P_1=\cdots=P_r$. Appendix A.1 does reuse the same field inverse and includes final XOR whitening. Our round convention adds one final public application, which preserves TV. [Liu–Tessaro–Vaikuntanathan, 2021](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/507.pdf)
- **The inverse and the jump at four.** Jain et al. analyze one repeated patched inverse, using approximations by fractional linear transformations. They obtain sharp order-of-growth full-permutation bounds on the reachable sign class and give a large lower bound already for four-wise approximation. Their §4.1, especially Lemmas 4.3–4.4, is the closest antecedent for the three-round triple certificate. We found no explicit three-round triple upper bound in the September 2025 full version. The efficient projection onto triples follows from sharp three-transitivity. [Jain et al., CRYPTO 2026, full version](https://eprint.iacr.asia/2024/1795.pdf)
- **What happens with other round structures.** Liu, Pelecanos, Tessaro and Vaikuntanathan use layout graphs to encode equality constraints for multiple SPN evaluations, a candidate tool for our triple collision calculation. Their main random-S-box result uses independently sampled secret S-boxes. Pelecanos’s separate master’s thesis includes an erratum on a proposed general $O(t)$-round MiMC result. The author records that statement as open. The 2026 work of Dujmovic, Pelecanos and Tessaro also separates limited independence from general pseudorandomness for compositions of local random permutations. [Layout graphs, CRYPTO 2023](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/083) · [MIT thesis](https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/144912) · [Author’s erratum listing](https://apelecan.github.io/) · [Dujmovic–Pelecanos–Tessaro](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2282)

## Counting, geometry and algorithms

- **Blocks and typical groups.** Caranti, Dalla Volta and Sala identify coset blocks in translation-based ciphers. Babai and Hayes show that adjoining a uniform permutation to a fixed group with few common fixed points almost surely generates the alternating or symmetric group. Applied to translations this already yields qualitative typical mixing simultaneously through order $N-2$. Our failure probability estimate uses Maróti’s bound on the order of primitive groups and Pyber–Shalev’s count of primitive actions. Our finite recurrence uses Möbius inversion on invariant partitions and decomposes nonlinear factors. [Caranti et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4135) · [Babai–Hayes](https://www.cs.unm.edu/~hayes/papers/OneRandom/OneRandom.pdf) · [Quantitative sources and proof](GROUP_COUNTS.md) · [Finite count and congruence references](FIVE_BIT_COUNT.md)
- **Affine geometry and one-step energy.** Bending and Fon-Der-Flaass give the crooked-function framework underlying the hyperplane certificate. Li, Meidl, Polujan, Pott, Riera and Stănică relate the count of vanishing affine planes to the differential spectrum in Theorem II.3. Accordingly, expressing one-step squared mass using such planes has a published antecedent. Reusing a random $P$ forces correlations in $T(P)^2$ that a one-step identity does not estimate. [Crooked functions](https://doi.org/10.37236/1372) · [Vanishing flats](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01941) · [Repeated-table calculation](RATES_AND_LITERATURE.md)
- **Efficient decision algorithms.** Furst, Hopcroft and Luks give the polynomial-time group operations underlying the exact all-orders decision. Unger gives practical giant-group recognition via cycle structures. In the 10,000-table four-bit sample a cheap prime-cycle witness resolved 9,437 tables directly. Other relevant methods impose small-base or random-group-element assumptions. The algorithm review records these dependencies alongside the project’s Walsh-transform pairwise screen. [Group algorithms, examples and comparisons](ALGORITHMIC_PRIOR_ART.md) · [Unger](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.09431) · [Walsh screen proof](../FAST_TABLE_PROOF.md)
- **Table equivalence and input access.** Saarinen exhaustively studies four-bit S-box properties under useful equivalences. Independent input/output relabelings can change a repeated-table walk. Our exhaustive rate census only uses equivalences preserving the walk at every round. The single-permutation Even–Mansour literature also studies public-table reuse, often with bounded oracle queries. Our criterion conditions on the whole realized table, permits the worst input to depend on it, and measures TV over fresh keys. [Saarinen](https://eprint.iacr.org/2011/218) · [Canteaut–Roué](https://www.iacr.org/archive/eurocrypt2015/90560131/90560131.pdf) · [Yu et al., TCC 2023](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1355)

## Outstanding work

1. **Typical triple rates.** Four rounds are optimal at TV $1/4$. Determine the limiting distance after three. The first three factorial moments give bounds of $1/3$ and $1/2$ up to vanishing error. Proving convergence of all fixed factorial moments to one would give a Poisson occupancy law and the candidate limit $e^{-1}$. The explicit inverse gives a useful three-round comparison at the same tuple order.
2. **The exact six-bit count.** The recurrence already counts every permutation preserving a proper coset partition. The outstanding part is counting primitive groups that fail pairwise mixing, including overlaps among their embeddings. The six-bit product example is a concrete test for any proposed formula. [Example](../LARGER_BITS.md#a-six-bit-counterexample)
3. **Efficient finite-round certificates for broader structured permutations.** The group classifier decides eventual convergence and the published inverse lower bound limits rates at four inputs. A certificate that verifies a useful round count for triples or quadruples of a broad class of succinctly described maps would address another part of the original assignment. For quadruples the starting tuple can itself lie in a binary affine plane, so a typical-table theorem must also control this class of starts. [Compact-input limits](../CIRCUIT_INPUT.md)

**Review scope, 18 November 2026.** We followed citations in the assignment’s paper and the inverse-walk paper, checked primary publications and author copies, and searched across differential distributions, Markov ciphers, decorrelation, key-alternating permutations, crooked functions, vanishing flats, block systems, random generation, and permutation-group algorithms. The two companion reviews give detailed bibliographies by question. Project derivation means a proof is available here and publication priority is unresolved.
