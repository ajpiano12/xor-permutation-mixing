# How often does a repeated random permutation fail to mix?

Take one uniformly random permutation $P$ of the $N=2^n$ points of
$V=\mathbb F_2^n$ and reuse it in every round, with fresh independent XOR keys.
Let $f_t(n)$ be the probability that some starting ordered tuple of $t$ distinct
points fails to converge to uniform on all such tuples. All probabilities in
this note are over the choice of $P$. The question concerns eventual
convergence, rather than a prescribed number of rounds.

**Result.** Uniformly over $2\le t\le N-2$, as $n\to\infty$,

$$
f_t(n)\sim \frac{2(N-1)}{\binom N{N/2}}
       \sim \sqrt{2\pi}\,N^{3/2}2^{-N}.
$$

Conditional on failure, the probability that $P$ preserves at least one
parity partition tends to one. This explains the scale change visible from
three bits to four. At five bits the probability of pairwise failure is between
$1.0314758815164\times10^{-7}$ and $1.0314762567980\times10^{-7}$,
roughly one permutation in 9.69 million. These decimal endpoints have been
rounded outwards. A separate [exact five-bit count](FIVE_BIT_COUNT.md)
now determines the probability inside this interval. The finite interval is proved below independently of the
asymptotic error estimate.

## Prior art and scope

Babai and Hayes prove that adjoining one uniform permutation to a group with
$o(N)$ common fixed points generates $A_N$ or $S_N$ with probability tending
to one. The translations act transitively. Their theorem, together with the
orbit argument below, already gives $f_t(n)=o(1)$ simultaneously for
$2\le t\le N-2$. Eberhard, Ford and Koukoulopoulos estimate a different
event in which the transitive subgroup containing a random permutation can be
chosen without fixing its translation group. The fixed translations are
essential to the much smaller probability computed here.
[Babai–Hayes, Theorem 1 (2006)](https://www.cs.unm.edu/~hayes/papers/OneRandom/OneRandom.pdf) ·
[Eberhard–Ford–Koukoulopoulos, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3 (2016)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.01068)

The proof combines the published key-alternating coset obstruction with
two published bounds on primitive permutation groups. Li gives a structural
classification of primitive groups containing a regular abelian subgroup.
Bounds on group orders and numbers of group actions suffice here, without
cataloguing all those types or assuming that the given translations are the
socle of an affine group. The quantitative equivalent, the quotient expansion,
and the finite interval are project derivations. Independent publication
priority remains unverified. [Caranti–Dalla Volta–Sala, Corollary 3.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4135) ·
[Li (2003)](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0024611503014266) ·
[Maróti, Corollary 1.1](https://users.renyi.hu/~maroti/primitive.pdf) ·
[Pyber–Shalev (1997)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021869396968180)

## Why a fixed parity split dominates

Let $T$ be the group of binary translations, $G=\langle T,P\rangle$, and
$H$ the normal closure of $T$ in $G$. The walk mixes on ordered $t$-tuples
exactly when $H$ is $t$-transitive. Grouping rounds into blocks of length
equal to the order of $P$ gives this criterion. The quotient $G/H$ is cyclic,
generated by the image of $P$. Consequently $G'$ lies in $H$. If $G$
contains $A_N$ and $N\ge5$, the perfect group $A_N$ lies in $H$, and $A_N$
is transitive on ordered distinct $t$-tuples for $t\le N-2$. Two unused
points allow either parity of a completion. [Full group-and-walk proof](../LARGER_BITS.md#an-exact-criterion-for-every-bit-length)

Conversely, if $G$ is imprimitive, its blocks are the cosets of a proper
nonzero binary subspace $W$. Translations force this form: translating the
block of zero by one of its elements gives the same block. Preserving the
partition leaves all nonzero differences in $W$ trapped there. Pairwise
nonmixing implies nonmixing on $t$-tuples by taking a marginal. We have
sandwiched failure between imprimitivity and imprimitivity plus the event
that $G$ is primitive and excludes $A_N$.

A nonzero linear functional $h$ defines a parity split into two halves.
Its stabilizer allows independently permuting each half and exchanging
the halves. Its probability is $2/\binom{N}{N/2}$. There are $N-1$ such
splits. For two distinct masks, their joint map to two bits has four equal
fibers. Keeping both splits fixes the two output bits up to a translation,
giving probability $4((N/4)!)^4/N!$. Thus the sum of the single probabilities
approximates their union with absolute error at most

$$
\binom{N-1}{2}\,4\,\frac{((N/4)!)^4}{N!}
=\exp(-N\log4+O(\log N)).
$$

For a fixed subspace of codimension $d$ with $q=2^d$ cosets of size $b=N/q$,
the full stabilizer has $q!(b!)^q$ elements. Here is a bound useful for every
fixed integer $k\ge1$. The probability that *some nonzero proper* subspace of
codimension $d\ge k+1$ has its cosets preserved is at most

$$
\exp\bigl(-(k+1)N\log 2+o_k(N)\bigr).
$$

Indeed, for $q\le\sqrt N$, factorial estimates give
$\log(q!(b!)^q/N!)=-N\log q+O(q\log N)$. For $q>\sqrt N$, use
$N!\ge(N/e)^N$, $b!\le b^b$ and $q!\le q^q$. The log probability is at most
$N-(N-q)\log q\le N-\tfrac14N\log N$, since $b\ge2$.
There are at most $(n+1)2^{n^2}$ subspaces, by counting ordered spanning
sets of each dimension. This factor contributes $o(N)$ to the exponent.

## Primitive exceptions and periodic chains

The number of conjugacy classes of primitive subgroups of $S_N$ is at most
$N^{c\mu(N)}$ for an absolute constant $c$, where $\mu(N)$ is the largest
exponent in the prime factorization of $N$. Here $\mu(N)=n$. Every primitive
group $K$ of degree $N$ excluding $A_N$ has $|K|<50N^{\sqrt N}$.
[Pyber–Shalev](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021869396968180) ·
[Maróti, Corollary 1.1(ii)](https://users.renyi.hu/~maroti/primitive.pdf)

Fix one such representative $K$. For a bijection $g$ satisfying
$g^{-1}Tg\le K$, the images under conjugation of the $n$ standard
translations have at most $|K|^n$ choices. For each choice, two conjugators
differ by a member of the centralizer of $T$. A map commuting with every
translation is determined by its image of zero, so this centralizer has size
$N$. Each distinct conjugate $gKg^{-1}$ containing $T$ arises from at least
$|K|$ conjugators. Multiplying the resulting number of conjugates by $|K|$
bounds the number of possible $P$ for this class by $N|K|^n$.

Summing over classes and dividing by $N!$ gives

$$
\Pr(G\text{ primitive},\ A_N\nleq G)
\le \frac{N^{cn}\,N\,(50N^{\sqrt N})^n}{N!}
=\exp(-N\log N+O(N)).
$$

An imprimitive $G$ gives a disconnected difference graph. A connected periodic
chain therefore belongs to the primitive exceptional event just bounded.
Combining these estimates with the two-half calculation proves the main
equivalent and the conditional statement. Stirling's formula supplies
the constant $\sqrt{2\pi}$.

At $t=N-1$ and $t=N$ there is a separate exact boundary. For $n\ge2$
every translation is even, so every round has sign $\mathrm{sgn}(P)$.
At time $r$ the parity of the full permutation is fixed. Its action on an
ordered $(N-1)$-tuple determines the last image as well, leaving half
of the targets unreachable at each time. Thus $f_{N-1}(n)=f_N(n)=1$.

## A fixed-order expansion

For sharper estimates let $[n\;d]_2$ denote the binary Gaussian binomial
coefficient, the number of subspaces of codimension $d$. Define integers

$$
c_d=(2^d)!-\sum_{j=1}^{d-1}
[d\;j]_2 c_j\,((2^{d-j})!)^{2^j}\qquad(d\ge1).
$$

For every fixed $k\ge1$, uniformly in $2\le t\le N-2$,

$$
\left|f_t(n)-\frac{1}{N!}\sum_{d=1}^k
[n\;d]_2 c_d\,((N/2^d)!)^{2^d}\right|
\le \exp\bigl(-(k+1)N\log2+o_k(N)\bigr).
$$

In particular $c_1=2$, $c_2=0$, and $c_3=32{,}256$. The contributions
on the four-coset scale cancel. The first nonzero correction to the leading
expression is $[n\;3]_2\,32{,}256\,((N/8)!)^8/N!$.

**Proof of the coefficients.** Use inclusion–exclusion for the union of
events that $P$ preserves cosets of a proper nonzero space $W$. Index a
nonempty family of distinct $W$ by its intersection $I$. Preserving each
partition preserves their common refinement, the cosets of $I$. For fixed
$I\ne0$ of codimension $d$, families with this intersection correspond to
families of proper subspaces of $V/I$ having zero intersection, including
the possibility of the zero subspace. If $s$ quotient permutations preserve
all members of one family, its full intersection event has
$s\,((N/2^d)!)^{2^d}$ labeled permutations, by arbitrary bijections inside
the fibers. Thus the signed contribution for this $I$ is a universal
coefficient $c_d$ times this fiber factor.

To evaluate $c_d$, apply the same inclusion–exclusion in dimension $d$ to
all proper subspaces **including zero**. The union contains every permutation
because any permutation preserves the singleton partition. Group by
intersection again. The term with zero intersection is $c_d$, so subtracting
the terms of codimension $j<d$ from $(2^d)!$ gives the displayed recurrence.

**Proof of the error.** Truncate the signed indicator to families whose
intersection has codimension at most $k$. Its expectation is precisely the
displayed sum. For each table its absolute value is at most the number of
retained families. For each possible intersection $I$, a retained family is
a subset of the subspaces of its quotient of dimension at most $k$. Thus the
number of retained families is at most $(n+1)2^{n^2}$ times a constant
depending only on $k$. Its logarithm is $o_k(N)$.

The truncated and true indicators agree unless the intersection of *all*
preserved proper nonzero subspaces has codimension greater than $k$. For
$n>2k$, this exceptional event implies the existence of a preserved
**nonzero** subspace of codimension at least $k+1$. Indeed, if one of the
original subspaces has such codimension we are done. Otherwise take
successive intersections until their codimension first exceeds $k$. At that
step it is at most $2k<n$, and intersections of preserved partitions still
give preserved coset partitions. The previous high-codimension bound now
controls the expected truncation error, including multiplication by the
number of retained families. The primitive exception is smaller at every
fixed order. This proves the claimed expansion.

## Five bits with finite bounds

Let $B_n$ count permutations preserving at least one parity split. The
invariant masks form a binary vector space. Fixing a $d$-space of masks lets
$P$ translate the $2^d$ fibers of their joint map and permute within them.
Inverting on the subspace lattice gives the exact integer

$$
B_n=\sum_{d=1}^n (-1)^{d+1}2^{d(d-1)/2}[n\;d]_2\,
2^d\,((N/2^d)!)^{2^d}.
$$

Equivalently, this follows by downward subtraction of the numbers of larger
spaces containing a given mask space. At $n=5$ the dimension-specific
criterion says that each failure is affine or preserves some nontrivial
coset partition. An affine map with reducible linear part already preserves
one. Irreducible linear parts have order 31 since $31$ is prime. There are
six Frobenius orbits on the 30 nonidentity elements of $\mathbb F_{32}^*$.
Each gives a conjugacy class of $|\mathrm{GL}(5,2)|/31$ matrices, since its
centralizer is the group of nonzero field multiplications. Allowing the
32 translations yields exactly $J=61{,}931{,}520$ connected periodic
permutations. Their irreducibility makes them disjoint from the parity family.
[Five-bit criterion and the degree-32 classification](../LARGER_BITS.md#five-bits)

Consequently $32! f_2(5)$ lies between $B_5+J$ and

$$
B_5+J+\sum_{d=2}^4[5\;d]_2\,(2^d)!\,((32/2^d)!)^{2^d}.
$$

The upper bound allows overlaps. The separate congruence-lattice recurrence
resolves the exact count. This interval alone places the fraction of failures explained by preserved parity above
99.99996%. With uniform independent samples of five-bit tables, a sample of
10,000 has probability below 0.001032 of encountering even one nonmixer, by
a union bound.
